  # Agentic Field Service Dispatch & Route Optimization

  ## Problem Statement
  Service-based and field operation owners (e.g., Carlos the Handyman, Fatima managing catering deliveries) struggle with fragmented logistics. Currently, OHC handles booking and quotes, but once a service is confirmed, the owner is left to manually sequence their day, estimate travel times, and notify clients of their arrival status. This disconnect leads to inefficient routing, missed appointments, and frustrated customers. Traditional scheduling software either lacks intelligent geographic routing or is too complex (and disconnected from the core OHC sales/booking pipeline) for a single-person SMB.

  ## Research Report
  - **Market Context**: Platforms like Shopify do not handle field services. Jobber and Housecall Pro are the industry standards for field service management (FSM), but they are often overly complex, requiring manual dispatching and lacking an integrated "Agentic" approach.
  - **The OHC Opportunity**: By extending OHC's Operations Agent and Customer Success Agent, we can fully automate the daily dispatch loop. The Operations Agent can autonomously sequence the day based on geographic data, while the Customer Success Agent handles "I'm on my way" texts.
  - **Competitor Gaps**:
    - *Housecall Pro/Jobber*: Excellent FSM but heavily reliant on manual owner input for route planning.
    - *Wix/Squarespace*: No native geographic routing or dispatch capabilities.

  ## Product-use evidence
  Persona: Carlos the Handyman.
  UI Flow Attempted: Logged into the local OHC stack running on Docker. Navigated to the confirmed booking section and tried to view today's schedule and dispatch route.
  CUJ Gap Observed: The product currently shows bookings but lacks any route sequencing or travel time estimates. There is no automated workflow to notify a customer that the service provider is "en route". Carlos would have to switch to Google Maps and manually text the customer.
  Post-fix UX: Carlos should see a "Today's Route" card with an optimized sequence and travel times directly in the OHC mobile view, with a 1-tap "Start Next Job" button.

  ## Design Doc
  ### Architecture Diagram
  ```mermaid
  graph TD
      A[Confirmed Booking/Order] --> B(Operations Agent: The Manager)
      B --> C{Route Optimization Engine}
      C -->|Calculate Travel Time| D[External Maps API / Cache]
      C -->|Update| E[Daily Dispatch Ledger]
      E --> F[Mobile App: Today's Route Feed 375px]
      F -->|Owner Taps 'Start Route'| G(Customer Success Agent: The Ambassador)
      G -->|Dispatch ETA SMS| H[Customer Phone]
  ```

  ### Mobile UX Flow (375px First)
  1. **Morning Briefing**: Carlos opens OHC at 8:00 AM. The primary card is "Today's Route" generated by the Operations Agent. It lists his 3 jobs in an optimized sequence, including travel times.
  2. **En Route Action**: Carlos taps "Head to Job 1".
  3. **Agent Coordination**: Behind the scenes, the Customer Success Agent automatically texts the client: "Hi! Carlos is on his way and should arrive in roughly 15 minutes."
  4. **Job Completion**: Upon tapping "Complete Job", the Finance Agent prepares the final invoice and the map seamlessly highlights the route to Job 2.

  ### AI Agent Integration Points
  - **Operations Agent (The Manager)**: Triggers nightly or upon new urgent bookings. Uses geographic clustering and travel-time estimates to sequence the day's `Booking` records into an optimized `ServiceRoute`.
  - **Customer Success Agent (The Ambassador)**: Listens for state changes on the `ServiceRoute` (e.g., status changed to `EN_ROUTE`) and drafts/sends personalized ETA notifications based on the customer's preferred communication channel.

  ### Key Design Decisions
  - **Offline-Tolerant Route Caching**: The mobile client must cache the daily route sequence so the owner can view their next stop even in poor cell service areas.
  - **Dynamic Buffer Times**: The Operations Agent must account for flexible buffer times (e.g., parking, setup) between jobs, learning from past job completion durations.

  ## Implementation Prompt
  **Feature Name**: OHC Agentic Route Optimization
  **Target Persona**: Carlos the Handyman
  **Outcome**: Carlos opens his OHC app in the morning to find his jobs automatically ordered by location. When he taps "Start Next Job", the system automatically texts the customer his ETA.

  **Next Actions**:
  1. Define the `ServiceRoute` and `JobStop` data models in PostgreSQL, linked to existing `Booking` records.
  2. Implement a basic geographic sequencing service (integrating with an external mapping/routing API) managed by the Operations Agent.
  3. Update the Mobile App Feed (375px) to display a "Today's Route" card with "Start/En Route/Complete" action buttons.
  4. Extend the Customer Success Agent to listen for `EN_ROUTE` events and trigger outbound customer SMS notifications.

  **Acceptance Criteria**:
  - Playwright E2E test verifying a real route is displayed on the feed, seeded through the database (no mock UI data).
  - Clicking "Head to Job" updates the status and triggers a real network call through the test-mode SMS adapter.
