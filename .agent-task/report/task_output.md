  # Mission Queue Protocol: AI-Agentic Dynamic Quote Generation & Auto-Deposit Workflow

  ## Problem Statement
  Service operators like **Carlos (Handyman, 42)** manage their business largely through SMS, phone calls, and word of mouth via an Android phone. When a lead requests a service, the manual process of typing out an estimate, following up, sending a payment link for a deposit, and securing a calendar slot creates a high friction barrier, leading to lost leads and double bookings. Traditional platforms like Shopify or Wix are too heavyweight, requiring desktop configurations for simple service flows, while tools like Square or Calendly require piecing together disjointed apps. The gap is a unified, mobile-first agentic workflow where the AI assistant takes an inbound message, instantly generates a quote with an integrated deposit link, and reserves a calendar slot invisibly.

  ## Research Report
  ### Track 1 & 2: Market Mapping & Deep-Dive Competitor Audit
  Our dynamic research across 2025 SMB SaaS platforms reveals:
  - **Square & HubSpot:** Offer robust invoicing and quoting tools, but they rely heavily on manual data entry by the user. The "AI" features are mostly text-generation, not end-to-end task execution.
  - **Jobber / Housecall Pro:** Excellent for field services, but often feel too enterprise-oriented and complex for a solo operator like Carlos. Their setup flows assume an administrative office worker, not a 375px mobile-first operator.
  - **Shopify & Durable:** Durable is great for rapid site creation but lacks deep field-service operation flows. Shopify is ill-suited for bespoke service quoting with variable deposit amounts.

  **The OHC Differentiator:** The OHC platform is built on an Assistant-First Shell. The missing architectural capability is a real-time, multi-agent negotiation pipeline where the *Operations Assistant* checks availability, the *Sales & Revenue Assistant* drafts the quote/deposit request, and the *Customer & Relationship Assistant* handles the conversational SMS/WhatsApp interface—all converging into a single unified action card for the owner to approve on a 375px screen.

  ## Design Doc

  ### Architecture Design
  To support this capability, we need to introduce a unified `Proposal` entity integrated deeply with our `Ledger`, `Booking`, and `Agent` systems.

  ```mermaid

      QuoteService -->|Draft Created| OwnerFeed

      PaymentService -->|Stripe Link| Customer
  ```

  ### Mobile UX Flow
  1. **Notification:** Carlos receives a notification: "New lead: Fix leaking sink."
  2. **Triage Feed:** He opens the app to the 375px Assistant-First Shell. The top card shows the lead details, along with a pre-drafted quote generated by the Sales Agent based on his historical pricing for similar jobs.
  3. **One-Tap Approval:** The card features a clear breakdown: $150 Estimate, $50 Deposit. Carlos hits "Approve & Send."
  4. **Invisible Execution:** The system sends an SMS with a tracked Stripe Payment Link. Simultaneously, Redis Redlock temporarily holds the proposed time slot in his calendar for 30 minutes to prevent double booking.

  ### AI Agent Integration Points
  - **Memory Injection:** The Sales Agent queries tenant-scoped vector memory to identify the historical price Carlos charged for "leaking sink" repairs.
  - **Distributed Locks:** Redis Redlock ensures that while the quote is pending with the customer, the calendar slot is soft-locked. If the deposit isn't paid within the timeout, the lock expires.
  - **Observable Handoffs:** The transition from the Sales Agent drafting the quote to the Payment Service generating the Stripe link is recorded in the immutable audit log.

  ### Key Design Decisions
  - **Zero-Trust Identity:** Service calls between the Sales Agent and Payment Service must authenticate via SPIFFE/SPIRE.
  - **Mobile-First UX:** The quote approval UI must avoid spreadsheet-like line item editors by default, relying on AI-generated summaries with an "Advanced Settings" toggle for manual edits.
  - **Eventual Consistency & Webhooks:** Stripe webhook handlers must use idempotency keys and retry logic to finalize the quote and hard-book the calendar slot upon deposit payment.
